The flight home is a ritual — the overweight suitcase negotiation at JFK, the cheer (or the quiet exhale) when wheels touch Timehri. Here's the practical guide to Guyana's front door, for the annual pilgrimage or the [bigger move home](/articles/guyana-remigrant-scheme-guide/).

## Getting there: the routes that matter

**Cheddi Jagan International Airport (GEO)** at Timehri handles the diaspora corridors. The recurring cast of carriers:

- **Caribbean Airlines** — the workhorse for **New York (JFK)** and **Toronto**, frequently routing via Port of Spain
- **American Airlines** — service from **Miami** and at times New York, connecting the whole US network
- **JetBlue** — direct **New York** service that made the northeast run competitive
- Regional connections knit Guyana to Barbados, Trinidad, and the islands for onward travel

Routes and schedules shift with seasons and airline strategy — treat this as the shape of the market and check current schedules when booking. A second airport note: **Eugene F. Correia International (Ogle)**, on Georgetown's edge, handles regional hops and domestic flights to the interior — Kaieteur trips leave from there, not Timehri.

### The booking rhythm

Fares to Guyana move on a diaspora calendar:

- **Christmas is the Everest** — mid-December through early January sells out **months** ahead at premium prices; book by September if you can
- **August** (school holidays, weddings) runs hot as well
- **Late January to early June** is the value window
- Fare alerts on the JFK and Toronto routes catch the occasional sales that do happen

## Landing: immigration and customs

**Immigration:** Guyanese citizens should ideally travel on a [current Guyanese passport](/articles/renew-guyana-passport-abroad/). US, Canadian, and UK passports have generally entered visa-free for short stays. Born in Guyana but naturalized abroad? You remain Guyanese — dual citizenship is permitted — and carrying proof of Guyanese birth smooths any questions.

**Customs:** personal effects and standard traveller allowances pass freely; where returnees get questioned is **quantities of new goods** — a suitcase of tagged clothing or six phones reads as commercial. For genuine household quantities, [the barrel](/articles/shipping-barrel-to-guyana-cost/) is the right vehicle, and for a permanent return, the [remigrant scheme](/articles/guyana-remigrant-scheme-guide/) is the framework. Check the GRA's current allowances if you're pushing limits, especially at Christmas.

Have your **TIN** handy if you're collecting cargo in the same trip — [it unlocks most official counters](/articles/guyana-tax-rules-remigrants/).

## The last 40 kilometres

CJIA sits at **Timehri, about 40 km south of Georgetown** down the East Bank corridor — **45 minutes to well over an hour** as traffic dictates, and the corridor is busy with boom-era construction.

- **Licensed airport taxis** are the standard: confirm the fare or rate sheet before setting off
- **Arriving family** remains the sweetest option Guyana offers
- **Night arrivals:** most diaspora flights land in the evening; arrange your pickup in advance rather than negotiating tired at midnight
- Heading beyond Georgetown (Berbice, Essequibo)? Plan the onward leg for morning unless someone's driving you through

## The send-off checklist

- Passport validity — **six months** is the safe rule; [renew early](/articles/renew-guyana-passport-abroad/)
- Baggage allowances — the barrel exists so your suitcase doesn't have to weigh 32 kg
- Cash — some US dollars convert easily; [know the rate](/convert/100-usd-to-gyd/) before the cambio conversation
- Phone — local SIMs (GTT or Digicel) at the airport or in town beat roaming charges
- Travel insurance if your health coverage [stops at the border](/articles/retiring-in-guyana-foreign-pension/)

Walk out of arrivals, feel that first wall of warm air, and you're home. The rest of this site handles everything after that.
