Almost every immigration process — US green cards, Canadian PR, UK visas, even some job applications — eventually asks for a **police clearance certificate** (sometimes called a police certificate or certificate of character) from every country you've lived in. If you grew up in Guyana, that means a certificate from the Guyana Police Force, and getting one from Brooklyn or Toronto takes some logistics.

## Who issues it

Police clearances come from the **Criminal Records Office of the Guyana Police Force**, headquartered at **Eve Leary, Georgetown**. There's no online application system, which is why applying from abroad revolves around one question: who's physically handing in your paperwork?

## The two routes from overseas

### 1. A representative in Guyana (the usual route)

Most overseas applicants authorize someone they trust in Guyana — a relative, friend, or attorney — to apply and collect on their behalf. The typical package you send them:

- A **written authorization** naming your representative
- **Certified fingerprints** — taken at a police station or accredited fingerprinting service where you live (call your local station; taking prints for foreign clearances is routine in the US, Canada, and UK)
- Copies of your **passport bio page** and, if asked, your Guyanese birth certificate
- Any **former names** (marriage, deed poll) with supporting documents
- The **fee** — modest, but confirm the current amount before sending funds

Your representative lodges everything at Eve Leary, pays, and collects the certificate when it's ready.

### 2. Through a Guyana consulate or embassy

Some Guyana missions abroad assist with police clearance applications or can direct you to the current procedure — worth a call to the consulate in New York or Toronto before recruiting a cousin for the job. Procedures change, and the mission will know the current fingerprints and fee requirements.

## Timing: start before you're asked twice

From abroad, budget **several weeks to a couple of months** end to end: getting fingerprints locally, mail to Guyana, processing in Georgetown, and return shipping. Immigration processes like the US National Visa Center or IRCC will ask for a certificate issued **recently** (often within the last 6–12 months), so don't order one years in advance — but the moment a checklist mentions it, start.

Use tracked courier services in both directions. A lost fingerprint card costs you a month.

## Common snags

- **Name mismatches** — the name on your fingerprint card and passport must line up with GRO records; unexplained variations trigger queries
- **Old addresses** — you may be asked for your last address in Guyana; dig it up before applying
- **Records from long ago** — if you left Guyana decades back, records checks can take longer; build in buffer
- **Sending cash** — don't; arrange the fee through your representative properly

## While you're gathering documents

Immigration checklists tend to arrive in clusters. If you need the police clearance, odds are you'll also need a fresh [GRO birth certificate](/articles/guyana-birth-certificate-from-abroad/) and a valid [Guyanese passport](/articles/renew-guyana-passport-abroad/) — start all three at once and save yourself serial waiting.
