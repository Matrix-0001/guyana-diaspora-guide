Whether you're in Georgetown or Brooklyn, the Guyana passport process trips people up on the same details — the wrong form, a birth-certificate problem, or the assumption that you can "renew" when Guyana actually makes you apply for a brand-new one. Here's the whole thing, start to finish, for applicants at home and abroad.

## First, the fact that surprises everyone: Guyana doesn't renew passports

There is no renewal sticker or extension. If your passport has **expired, or has fewer than six months of validity left**, you apply for an **entirely new electronic passport** — using the same form and largely the same documents as a first-time applicant. So when people search "renew Guyana passport," what they actually need is a **new e-passport application**. Since 2025 the passport is a biometric **e-passport**, and you choose and pay for either a **5-year** or **10-year** validity when you apply.

## The form you need: Form A

Every applicant — first-time, replacement, adult, or child — uses **Form A**, the official passport application form.

- Fill it out in **black ink** and **BLOCK CAPITALS**.
- Sign where required (children aged 10–17 sign their own form; under-10s don't sign).
- Complete **Section 7, the recommender section** — an eligible professional or long-standing acquaintance who has known you for several years vouches for your identity.

## Documents you'll need

Exact requirements vary slightly by office and by whether you're applying at home or abroad, but plan for:

- **Completed Form A** (with the recommender section signed)
- **Original birth certificate** from the General Register Office — it must show your parents' full names and nationalities
- **Valid National Identification Card** (for in-country applicants)
- Your **current or expired passport**, plus a **colour photocopy of the bio-data page**
- **Two identical passport photographs** meeting the specifications below
- **Marriage certificate or deed poll** if your name has changed
- A **statutory declaration** if your only proof of citizenship is the birth certificate, or to explain discrepancies
- The **fee**

A common instruction is that supporting documents be submitted **in duplicate** — bring both the original and a photocopy of each.

## Photo specifications

Get these done by a photographer who knows passport requirements — rejected photos are a frequent cause of delay:

- Size between **32mm × 26mm and 35mm × 45mm**
- **Plain white background** — no colours, patterns, or shadows
- **Neutral expression, mouth closed**
- **No glasses**, and no head coverings except for religious reasons (facial features must be visible)
- Taken within the **last 3 months**
- **Two identical, colour** prints

## Fees (2026)

Fees change, so treat these as a guide and confirm the current amount before you pay:

- **Applying inside Guyana:** around **G$12,000** for the e-passport, with the **10-year** validity costing more than the **5-year**, and an **expedited service** available for an additional fee.
- **Applying overseas** through a consulate: roughly **US$100** plus any mission handling costs, usually payable by money order.

## Where and how to apply — inside Guyana

Applications are handled by the **Central Immigration and Passport Office** in **Georgetown** (under the Ministry of Home Affairs), with regional passport offices in **Linden, Berbice, and Essequibo** so you don't have to travel to the capital.

- **Standard processing:** about **15 working days**.
- **Expedited processing:** typically **3–5 working days** for the additional fee.
- **Start online, finish in person.** You can begin your application and **book your appointment through the official passport portal at [pp.gpf.gov.gy](https://pp.gpf.gov.gy/#/)** — but the biometric e-passport still needs an in-person step for your photo, signature, and document check. There is no *fully* online passport.

## Booking your appointment

- **Inside Guyana:** book through the **official passport portal, [pp.gpf.gov.gy](https://pp.gpf.gov.gy/#/)**, which is also where you start the application.
- **From abroad:** book through your nearest mission — the [New York consulate](https://guyanaconsulatenewyork.org/), [Washington DC embassy](https://guyanaembassydc.org/appointments), and [Toronto consulate](https://guyanaconsulatetoronto.com/book-an-appointment/) all take appointments online.

All the phone numbers and booking links are on our [official contacts &amp; appointments page](/contacts/).

## Where and how to apply — from the US, Canada, or UK

If you live abroad, you apply through a Guyana diplomatic mission:

- **United States:** the Consulate General of Guyana in **New York**, or the Embassy of Guyana in **Washington, DC**
- **Canada:** the Consulate General in **Toronto** or the High Commission in **Ottawa**
- **United Kingdom:** the Guyana High Commission in **London**

Some missions accept applications by mail; others require or prefer in-person appointments, and consulates periodically run **outreach visits** to cities with large Guyanese communities (Miami, Atlanta, Scarborough, and others). Applications are **forwarded to Guyana** for production and then returned to the mission, so the whole cycle commonly takes **several weeks to a few months**. There is no same-day passport abroad — check your mission's current instructions and whether mail-in is accepted before doing anything else.

**No Guyana consulate in your city?** There are only a handful of missions, so you apply through the one that serves your region — not a local office. If you're in **Florida** or the wider US South, that usually means the **New York consulate** or the **Washington, DC embassy**, and it's worth watching for **Miami outreach events** that bring consular services closer to home. Applicants far from any mission typically use mail-in (where it's accepted) or catch an outreach visit rather than travelling to New York or Toronto.

## The birth-certificate problem (the #1 delay)

The single most common hold-up — at home and abroad — is the birth certificate: applicants who don't have their original GRO certificate, or whose certificate has an **error or a different name spelling** than their old passport. Replacement certificates and corrections come from the **General Register Office in Georgetown**, and from overseas that means either a relative in Guyana doing the legwork or a long mail process. If your certificate situation isn't clean, **fix it first** — see our guide to [getting a Guyana birth certificate](/articles/guyana-birth-certificate-from-abroad/) and the [GRO certificate forms](/articles/guyana-gro-certificates-birth-marriage-death/).

## Passports for children

- Children under 18 receive **5-year** passports.
- **Both parents should ideally be present**, or one parent must provide a signed **No Objection Letter** authorising the application.
- Children aged **10–17 sign** their own Form A; under-10s do not.
- Bring the child's **birth certificate** showing both parents.

## Practical tips

- **Start early.** Many airlines and countries require six months of validity — treat the six-month mark as already expired, and remember you're applying for a *new* passport, not a quick renewal.
- **Photocopy everything** and, if mailing, use tracked shipping both ways.
- **Dual citizens:** Guyana permits dual citizenship. If you were born in Guyana you generally remain a citizen after naturalising in the US, Canada, or UK — but Guyanese citizens are expected to enter Guyana on a Guyanese passport, one more reason to keep it current.
- **Genuinely urgent travel** (a death in the family): contact the passport office or mission directly — emergency travel documents may be possible in limited cases, but don't count on it.
