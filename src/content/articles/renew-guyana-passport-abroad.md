Your Guyanese passport expired years ago, you need to travel home for a funeral or a wedding, and suddenly it's urgent. Here's how renewal works from overseas — and why you should start **months** before you need to travel.

## Where to apply

You renew through Guyana's diplomatic missions:

- **United States:** the Consulate General of Guyana in New York, or the Embassy of Guyana in Washington, DC
- **Canada:** the Consulate General in Toronto or the High Commission in Ottawa
- **United Kingdom:** the Guyana High Commission in London

Some missions accept applications by mail; others require or prefer in-person appointments, and consulates periodically run **outreach visits** to cities with large Guyanese communities (Miami, Atlanta, Scarborough, etc.). Check your mission's current instructions before doing anything else — procedures change.

## What you'll typically need

Exact requirements vary by mission, but expect:

- Completed passport application form (from the mission's website)
- Your current or expired Guyanese passport
- Your **original Guyanese birth certificate** — this is the big one
- Passport-size photos meeting Guyana's specifications
- Marriage certificate or deed poll if your name has changed
- The fee, usually payable by money order

## The birth certificate problem

The single most common delay: applicants who don't have their original GRO birth certificate, or whose certificate has errors or a different name spelling than their old passport. Replacement certificates come from the **General Register Office in Georgetown**, and getting one from abroad means either a relative in Guyana doing legwork for you or a long mail process. If your certificate situation isn't clean, fix it **first**.

## How long it takes

Be realistic: applications are forwarded to Guyana, where passports are produced, then returned to the mission. Depending on the mission and time of year, the whole cycle commonly takes **several weeks to a few months**. There is no same-day renewal abroad.

If you have genuinely urgent travel (a death in the family), contact the mission directly — emergency travel documents may be possible in limited cases, but don't count on it.

## Practical tips

- **Renew early.** Many airlines and countries want six months of validity; treat the six-month mark as expired.
- **Photocopy everything** you mail, and use tracked shipping both ways.
- **Dual citizens:** Guyana permits dual citizenship, and if you were born in Guyana you generally remain a citizen after naturalizing elsewhere. Note that Guyanese citizens are expected to enter Guyana on a Guyanese passport where applicable — one more reason to keep it current.
- **Check for outreach events** — a consulate visit to your city can save you a trip to New York or Toronto.
