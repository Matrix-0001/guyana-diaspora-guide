Canada is home to one of the largest Guyanese communities in the world — Scarborough alone is practically a second Georgetown. But Canadian family sponsorship works very differently from the US system, and mixing up the two costs families years. Here's how it actually works.

## Who you can sponsor

As a **Canadian citizen or permanent resident** (18+), you can sponsor:

- **Your spouse or common-law partner** — the workhorse category, no annual cap
- **Dependent children** — generally under 22 and unmarried
- **Parents and grandparents** — but only through a limited annual program (more below)
- **Siblings: effectively no** — except narrow cases such as orphaned minor siblings

That last point surprises people used to the US system. There is no Canadian equivalent of the US F4 sibling category.

## Sponsoring a spouse or partner

The process: you file the sponsorship and permanent residence application together, IRCC assesses both you (eligibility, no disqualifying history, not on social assistance) and your spouse (admissibility, medicals, police clearances — including a [police clearance from Guyana](/articles/police-clearance-certificate-guyana/)).

- **Timeline:** IRCC's standard has hovered around 12 months for complete applications
- **Cost:** government fees run roughly CA$1,200 per sponsored adult — confirm current amounts on the IRCC fee list
- **No minimum income requirement** applies for sponsoring a spouse (unlike parents), though you must not be on social assistance other than disability
- **The relationship is the case.** IRCC scrutinizes genuineness: photos over time, communication history, joint finances, visits. Thin evidence is the top reason Guyanese spousal cases go sideways.

**Common-law counts** — 12 months of continuous cohabitation — which matters for couples who never formally married.

## Parents and grandparents: the PGP lottery vs. the Super Visa

The **Parents and Grandparents Program (PGP)** grants permanent residence, but intake is capped: in recent years IRCC has drawn invitations from a pool of interest-to-sponsor forms, and demand massively exceeds spots. You also need to meet a **minimum necessary income** for three consecutive tax years, proven by CRA notices of assessment.

Because winning a PGP invitation can take years, the practical route for most families is the **Super Visa**:

- Multi-entry, valid up to **10 years**
- Stays of up to **five years per entry**, with possible extensions
- Requires proof your income meets the threshold for your family size, a written commitment of support, and **private medical insurance** from an approved insurer
- Your parent remains a **visitor** — no healthcare coverage from the province, no path to PR by itself

For a retired parent in Georgetown who mainly wants long stretches with the grandchildren in Toronto, the Super Visa often serves the actual goal better and starts in months, not years.

## Documents from the Guyana side

Start early on the paper chase: GRO birth and marriage certificates (order them before you need them — see our [guide to getting documents from abroad](/articles/guyana-birth-certificate-from-abroad/)), the police clearance from Eve Leary, and a valid Guyanese passport. Document mismatches — spelling variations, missing father's name on a birth certificate — cause more delays than IRCC itself.

## The realistic picture

| Route | What it gets | Realistic timing |
|---|---|---|
| Spousal sponsorship | Permanent residence | ~1 year from complete filing |
| Dependent child | Permanent residence | Similar to spousal |
| PGP (parents) | Permanent residence | Years — invitation not guaranteed |
| Super Visa | Long visits, 10-yr multi-entry | A few months |
