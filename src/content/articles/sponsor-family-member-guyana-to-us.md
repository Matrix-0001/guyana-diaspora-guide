Sponsoring a parent, spouse, child, or sibling from Guyana is one of the most common goals in the diaspora — and one of the most misunderstood. Here's how the process actually works, and how long it really takes.

## Who can you sponsor?

If you're a **US citizen**, you can petition for:

- Your spouse, parents, and unmarried children under 21 — these are **immediate relatives**, with no annual visa cap
- Adult children (married or unmarried) and siblings — these fall into **family preference categories** with long queues

If you're a **green card holder**, you can only petition for your spouse and unmarried children.

## Realistic wait times

This is where most families get blindsided. The petition itself (Form I-130) takes months to process, but the visa queue is what determines the real wait:

| Relationship | Typical total wait |
|---|---|
| Spouse, parent, minor child of US citizen | Roughly 1–2 years |
| Unmarried adult child of US citizen (F1) | Several years |
| Spouse/minor child of green card holder (F2A) | Roughly 1–3 years |
| Married adult child of US citizen (F3) | 10+ years |
| Sibling of US citizen (F4) | Often 12–15+ years |

Check the current [State Department Visa Bulletin](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/legal/visa-law0/visa-bulletin.html) for exact dates — it moves monthly. For a quick answer for your situation, try our [sponsorship wait-time estimator](/tools/visa-wait-estimator/).

## The process in five steps

1. **File Form I-130** with USCIS, with proof of your status and your relationship (birth certificates, marriage certificate). Guyanese civil documents come from the General Register Office (GRO) in Georgetown — order them early, because getting corrected or re-issued certificates can take time.
2. **Wait for approval and priority date.** Your filing date becomes your relative's place in line.
3. **National Visa Center (NVC) stage.** Once a visa is available, you submit the DS-260 application, civil documents, and the **Affidavit of Support (I-864)** — you must show income around 125% of the federal poverty guideline for your household size, or find a joint sponsor.
4. **Interview at the US Embassy in Georgetown.** Guyanese applicants interview at the embassy on Young and Duke Streets, Kingston, Georgetown. They'll need the medical exam from an embassy-approved physician beforehand.
5. **Visa issued, then green card on arrival.**

## Common mistakes Guyanese families make

- **Name and date-of-birth mismatches** between birth certificates, passports, and the petition. Fix discrepancies with the GRO before filing, not after.
- **Assuming a visitor visa refusal hurts the petition.** It doesn't — the family petition is a separate track.
- **Letting the case go stale at NVC.** If you don't respond for a year, the case can be terminated.
- **Unreported name changes after marriage or deed poll** — disclose everything consistently.

## What it costs

Budget roughly US$1,200–1,500 in government fees per person (I-130 filing, DS-260 processing, I-864 review, medical exam), more if you use an attorney. For straightforward immediate-relative cases, many families file themselves; for complicated histories (prior deportations, overstays, criminal records), a licensed immigration attorney is worth every cent.
