Guyana drives on the left, loves a Toyota, and taxes engines like they owe it money. Whether shipping your well-kept vehicle from abroad or buying one to send home for family, here's how vehicle importation really works — and when it's the wrong move entirely.

## How the taxes stack

Vehicle imports face a **stack** of charges — import duty, excise tax, and VAT — assessed on the vehicle's value (CIF: cost, insurance, freight to Georgetown). The stack's height turns on three dials:

1. **Engine size** — the dominant factor: small engines (under ~1500cc) are treated gently; large engines (2000cc and up) are taxed heavily, historically to the point where **taxes rival or exceed the car's value**
2. **Age** — vehicles **over four years old** are assessed differently (flat-rate excise regimes have applied) than newer ones; the four-year line has long been the industry's pivot point
3. **Fuel type** — Guyana has offered **sharply reduced taxes on electric vehicles** and incentives for lower-emission choices; if you've been EV-curious, the tax table is genuinely persuasive

The rates get adjusted in national budgets, so treat any specific number you've heard as dated: before committing to anything, run your vehicle through our [vehicle import duty calculator](/tools/vehicle-import-calculator/) for an estimate, then get the binding figure from a **licensed customs broker** in Georgetown quoting on your exact vehicle (make, model, year, engine cc, value). That quote is the foundation of the whole decision.

## The big exception: the remigrant concession

If you're **returning to Guyana permanently**, the [remigrant scheme](/articles/guyana-remigrant-scheme-guide/) has offered duty and tax concessions on a vehicle you've **owned abroad for the required period** — often the difference between shipping your car and abandoning the idea. The conditions are real: prior ownership requirements, limits on the vehicle, and a **multi-year restriction on selling** after arrival, with the waived taxes clawed back if you breach. Read our full remigrant guide before building plans on the concession.

## The process, end to end

1. **Confirm the vehicle qualifies** — check current rules on age limits and specifications; Guyana's fleet is right-hand-drive
2. **Get the broker's quote** on total landed cost: purchase price + shipping + insurance + the tax stack + broker and port fees
3. **Ship** — RoRo (roll-on/roll-off) or containerized, from the US East Coast, Toronto/Halifax, or (very commonly for purchases) **direct from Japan** via the used-vehicle exporters
4. **Clear at the port** — your broker handles GRA assessment, you'll need your **TIN** ([get one early](/articles/guyana-tax-rules-remigrants/)), title documents, and the bill of lading
5. **Register and license** locally, insure, and drive

Budget time as generously as money: shipping, port handling, and assessment take weeks, and holiday-season port congestion is [as real for vehicles as for barrels](/articles/shipping-barrel-to-guyana-cost/).

## Import vs. buy local: the honest comparison

Guyana's dealers — and the used-Japanese-import pipeline — mean **buying locally is often simpler and surprisingly price-competitive** for common models, because dealers' bulk importing and the same tax stack are already baked into lot prices. The import-it-yourself route tends to win when:

- You **already own** a well-maintained vehicle whose history you trust — the used-car lottery is real everywhere
- You qualify for the **remigrant concession**
- You want an **EV or small-engine vehicle** the tax table favors
- You're sourcing something specific the local market doesn't carry

And buying local usually wins when the mission is simply "reliable wheels for mom in Berbice" — a dealer in Georgetown with a warranty beats six weeks of ocean freight and port runs, most days.

## The rules of the road (figuratively)

- **Never buy for import before the broker quote** — the tax stack has ambushed a thousand optimists
- **Declare honestly** — undervaluing invoices is fraud, and the GRA has seen every trick
- **Insure the transit** — ocean shipping mishaps happen
- **Match the paperwork to your name and TIN** exactly — [the name-consistency rule](/articles/guyana-birth-certificate-from-abroad/) follows you even onto the wharf

Done with eyes open, importing puts you in a car you trust on Guyanese roads. Done on vibes, it's the most expensive way to learn what an excise schedule is.
