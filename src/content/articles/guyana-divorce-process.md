Divorce is stressful enough without the process being a mystery. Here's how it works in Guyana in 2026 — including the **new no-fault option** introduced in 2024 — so you know what to expect before you speak to a lawyer. This is general information, not legal advice: for your own case, get proper counsel.

## The big change: no-fault divorce (2024)

For most of Guyana's history you had to prove your spouse did something wrong to get divorced. A **2024 amendment to the divorce law changed that**, adding a **no-fault ground**: *irreconcilable differences that have caused an irremediable breakdown of the marriage*. This means couples can now end a marriage without assigning blame — a significant shift that makes uncontested divorces simpler. The same reforms also modernised **maintenance**, which can now run in either direction between spouses rather than only from husband to wife.

## Grounds for divorce

You file on one of the recognised grounds:

- **Irreconcilable differences** (the new no-fault ground) — irremediable breakdown of the marriage
- **Adultery**
- **Cruelty**
- **Malicious desertion** for **two or more years**
- **Incurable insanity** (after a lengthy period, generally five years)
- Certain **criminal acts** by the respondent

**The three-year rule:** you generally cannot petition for divorce until you have been **married for at least three years**, unless the court accepts a case of exceptional hardship or depravity.

## Where you file

Divorce is a matter for the **High Court of the Supreme Court of Judicature**, with petitions filed at the **Victoria Law Courts in Georgetown**, or through the relevant **Family Court**. Because these are formal court filings — not a downloadable form you post off — the paperwork is almost always prepared by an attorney.

## The process, step by step

1. **Consult an attorney** (or Legal Aid) to confirm your grounds, your eligibility, and where to file.
2. **The petition is filed.** The spouse seeking the divorce (the **petitioner**) files a divorce petition setting out the marriage details, the ground relied on, and any claims about **children, property, or maintenance**.
3. **The petition is served** on the other spouse (the **respondent**), usually by a process server or registered post.
4. **The respondent acknowledges service.** Once served in Guyana, the respondent generally has about **eight days** to file an acknowledgment stating whether they intend to contest.
5. **The court proceeds** — uncontested matters move relatively quickly; contested ones over children, property, or maintenance take longer and may involve hearings.
6. **The decree is granted** and, once finalised, you receive the court order ending the marriage. Keep certified copies — you'll need them for remarriage, immigration, and name changes.

## Documents you'll typically need

Your attorney will tell you exactly what your grounds require, but commonly:

- Your **marriage certificate** (from the [GRO](/articles/guyana-gro-certificates-birth-marriage-death/))
- **Birth certificates** of any children of the marriage
- A **list of property and assets** where division is in issue
- **Evidence** supporting your ground for divorce
- **Proof of domicile / residence** connecting the case to Guyana

## If you can't afford a lawyer

The **Guyana Legal Aid Clinic**, at **185 Charlotte & King Streets, Georgetown**, provides free legal advice and representation to people who qualify. If money is the obstacle between you and starting the process, that is the place to begin. You'll find it and the other authorities on our [official contacts page](/contacts/).

## A note for overseas Guyanese

Whether you *should* divorce in Guyana or in your country of residence depends on where you were married, where you live now, and domicile rules — it isn't automatic. If you married in Guyana but live in the US, Canada, or UK, get advice from a Guyanese family law attorney about the right forum before filing anywhere.
