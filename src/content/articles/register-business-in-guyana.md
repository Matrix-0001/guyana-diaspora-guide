Guyana's oil-era economy has more people starting businesses than ever — from a market stall to a construction outfit to a diaspora-backed venture. The registration itself is straightforward once you know the two paths. Here's the overview.

## Who handles it: the DCRA

Business registration is done through the **Deeds and Commercial Registries Authority (DCRA)** — specifically its **Commercial Registry**, located at **Lot 1 High and Commerce Streets, Georgetown**. Forms and details are on the DCRA website, **[dcraguyana.org](https://dcraguyana.org/)**.

## First decision: business name or company?

This is the choice that matters most, because it changes your paperwork, cost, and legal exposure:

- **Registered business name (sole trader or partnership).** The simplest, cheapest route. You trade under a registered name, but **you and the business are legally the same** — meaning you're **personally liable** for its debts. Best for small operations and getting started quickly.
- **Incorporated company (limited liability).** A **separate legal entity** that shields your personal assets, but with more paperwork at registration and **ongoing obligations** (annual returns, records). Expected for larger ventures, anything taking on investment, or where limited liability matters.

If you're unsure, a business name is the usual starting point for a small local operation; a company is worth the extra effort once real money, partners, or investors are involved.

## The steps

1. **Do a name search.** Submit a name search to the DCRA to confirm your proposed name is **available** and not identical or confusingly similar to an existing one.
2. **Mind the name rules.** Your name must be unique, and it **cannot contain restricted words** like *Bank*, *Insurance*, or *Government* without special approval.
3. **Complete the registration form.** For a business name, the **Business Name Registration form** captures the business name, the **nature of the business**, the **principal place of business**, and the **name, nationality, and residence** of the proprietor or each partner. A company requires incorporation documents instead (articles, directors, registered office).
4. **Pay the fee and submit** at the Commercial Registry.
5. **Receive your certificate** of registration or incorporation — your proof the business legally exists.

Fees vary by registration type and change over time, so confirm the current amounts at the DCRA counter or website rather than relying on old figures.

## After you register: the GRA

Registration with the DCRA is only step one. You then **register with the Guyana Revenue Authority (GRA)** for a **Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN)** and to meet your tax obligations — income/corporation tax, and VAT if you cross the registration threshold. Most banks will ask for **both your DCRA certificate and your TIN** before opening a business account, so do this early. ## For diaspora investors

If you're setting up from overseas, most people work through a **local attorney or a trusted partner on the ground** to handle the DCRA filings, the registered office requirement, and GRA registration. Decide the business-name-vs-company question early, because converting later is more work than starting in the right structure. ## Where to go

- **DCRA (Commercial Registry):** Lot 1 High and Commerce Streets, Georgetown — [dcraguyana.org](https://dcraguyana.org/)
- **Guyana Revenue Authority (for your TIN):** +592 227-6060 / 227-8222, [gra.gov.gy](https://www.gra.gov.gy/), 200–201 Camp Street, Georgetown
