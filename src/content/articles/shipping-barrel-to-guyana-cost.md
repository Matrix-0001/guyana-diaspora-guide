The barrel is a diaspora institution. Whether it's foodstuff, clothes, or school supplies for September, here's what shipping one to Guyana actually costs — including the part everyone forgets: clearing it on the other end.

## The cost has three parts

1. **The barrel itself** — a new fiber or plastic barrel from a shipping company typically runs US$20–50 depending on size (the common sizes are roughly 45–77 gallons).
2. **Ocean freight** — from the US East Coast or Toronto to Georgetown, expect roughly **US$100–180 per barrel** depending on the shipper, size, and season. Door-to-door pickup costs more than dropping it off at the warehouse.
3. **Clearance in Guyana** — customs duty and wharf/handling charges your consignee pays when collecting at the wharf in Georgetown. For personal-effects barrels this is often modest, but it is **not zero**, and it varies with contents.

## Who ships to Guyana

The long-standing names in the trade include **Laparkan** and other Caribbean freight companies operating out of New York, New Jersey, Miami, and Toronto, plus smaller community shippers who consolidate barrels. When choosing:

- Ask for the **all-in price** — barrel, freight, fuel surcharges, and paperwork.
- Ask about **transit time** (commonly 2–4 weeks on the water, plus processing on both ends).
- Around **Christmas**, book early. The September (back-to-school) and December rushes are real, and cutoff dates for holiday arrival come sooner than you think.

## The Christmas barrel concession

The Government of Guyana has, in many years, announced a **seasonal duty concession on barrels** of personal items around the Christmas period, reducing the clearance cost for families. It's announced year by year — check the Guyana Revenue Authority (GRA) news before assuming it applies.

## What your consignee needs in Guyana

- A valid ID and usually a **TIN (taxpayer identification number)** to clear the barrel
- The shipping documents you send them (bill of lading / shipper's receipt)
- Patience — collection at the wharf can take a chunk of a day during peak season

## Packing tips that save money and grief

- **Declare honestly.** New items in quantity look commercial, and commercial cargo is taxed differently than personal effects.
- **No prohibited items** — check GRA's list; aerosols and certain foods cause problems.
- **Pack heavy items low**, wrap liquids in plastic bags, and fill every gap — you pay by the barrel, not the pound.
- **Write the consignee's name and phone number** on the barrel in permanent marker, exactly matching their ID.

## Is a barrel still worth it?

For bulk foodstuff, toiletries, and clothing, usually yes — the per-item cost beats retail prices in Georgetown for many goods. For one or two items, a courier service or simply sending money is often cheaper. Do the math on the *contents*, not just the freight.
