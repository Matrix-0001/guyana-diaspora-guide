Almost every official process in Guyana — a [passport](/articles/renew-guyana-passport-abroad/), [family sponsorship](/articles/sponsor-family-member-guyana-to-us/), a pension, a land transfer, a marriage — rests on a certificate from the **General Register Office (GRO)**. This is the practical guide to getting birth, marriage, and death certificates: the exact forms, the fees, and how to do it from Georgetown or from overseas.

## Who issues certificates: the GRO

Guyana's civil records — births, marriages, and deaths — are held by the **General Register Office**, part of the Ministry of Home Affairs, with its main office in Georgetown. The GRO publishes fillable application forms on its official website, **[gro.moha.gov.gy](https://gro.moha.gov.gy/)**, and you can reach it by email at **GROguyana@gmail.com** or by WhatsApp.

## Fees

- **Standard certificate (birth, marriage, or death):** **G$300**
- **Expedited service:** around **G$3,000** (about US$15) when you need it quickly

Confirm the current fee with the GRO before submitting, as charges change.

## How to apply inside Guyana

You have a few routes:

1. **Through a post office.** Download and complete the form, then submit it with the fee at **any local post office**. The certificate is mailed back to you, usually within **3–6 weeks**. This is the most convenient option if you're not in Georgetown.
2. **In person at the GRO** in Georgetown — often faster, and the place to go for corrections or complicated searches.
3. **Direct contact** — the GRO accepts queries and some requests by **email (GROguyana@gmail.com)** and **WhatsApp**; ask about current procedures.

**Details that speed up the search:** the more you can give, the faster and more accurate the result — full name at birth, date of birth, place of birth (village/town and region), parents' full names, and the mother's maiden name. If a previous certificate exists, the **registration or entry number** is the single biggest time-saver.

## Applying from overseas

If you live abroad and need a Guyanese certificate, the usual routes are:

- **A trusted relative in Guyana** applies and collects on your behalf — often the fastest and cheapest.
- **A Guyana consulate or embassy** — several missions accept certificate requests and forward them to the GRO. Expect to add international mailing time on top of the GRO's own processing.
- **The Overseas Registration form** if the birth or event you're registering actually happened outside Guyana.

For a fuller walkthrough of the from-abroad routes, see our guide to [getting a Guyana birth certificate from overseas](/articles/guyana-birth-certificate-from-abroad/).

## Marriage certificates: two different things

People conflate these, so to be clear:

- **Getting married** in Guyana requires the **Marriage Petition** and, where one party has been married before or is a visitor, the **Certificate of Non-Impediment** proving you're free to marry — plus the licence or banns process. See [getting married in Guyana](/articles/getting-married-in-guyana/).
- **The marriage certificate** is the record you obtain *after* the wedding, using the **Marriage Certificate application form** above — this is what immigration authorities and banks will ask for. Get several certified copies at once; you'll need them.

## Fixing errors on a certificate

A misspelled name or wrong date is the classic cause of a stalled passport or visa application, because the certificate no longer matches your other documents. Small errors are handled through the GRO's **Minor Correction** process using the correction form, often supported by a **statutory declaration** and evidence. Corrections take **longer than a straight reprint**, so if your certificate has a problem, start the correction **before** you file anything that depends on it.
