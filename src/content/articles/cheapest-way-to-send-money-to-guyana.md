If you send money home regularly, the difference between the cheapest and the most expensive option adds up fast. On a US$200 monthly transfer, choosing poorly can cost you US$150–300 a year in fees and exchange rate markups — money that should be reaching your family in Guyana.

## The two costs to watch

Every transfer service charges you in two ways:

1. **The upfront fee** — the number they show you. Usually US$0–15 depending on the service, amount, and payout method.
2. **The exchange rate markup** — the hidden cost. The Guyanese dollar trades at roughly G$208–210 per US dollar, but some services pay out at a noticeably worse rate and keep the difference. A 2% markup on US$500 is US$10 you never see itemized.

Always compare the **total Guyanese dollars your recipient actually gets**, not the advertised fee.

## Your main options

### Western Union

The most widely available option, with agent locations across Guyana including banks and retail agents in Georgetown, Berbice, Linden, and Essequibo. Fees vary a lot by how you pay and how the money is collected — paying by bank account and sending to a bank account is usually cheaper than cash-to-cash. Convenient, but check the exchange rate offered before you confirm.

### MoneyGram

Very similar footprint and pricing to Western Union in Guyana. It's worth checking both for every transfer, because whichever is cheaper changes with promotions and payout method.

### Ria Money Transfer

Often undercuts Western Union and MoneyGram on fees for cash pickup. Fewer agent locations, so confirm there's a convenient pickup point for your recipient first.

### Bank wire transfer

Sending directly to an account at Republic Bank, GBTI, Demerara Bank, or another Guyanese bank is secure but usually the **most expensive** option — US and Canadian banks commonly charge US$25–50 per outgoing international wire, plus intermediary bank fees. Only worth it for large amounts.

### Mobile wallet payout

Mobile money services in Guyana (such as GTT's MMG+) allow recipients to receive funds on their phone and pay bills without visiting an agent. Availability through international senders is still growing — check whether your service supports it.

## Rules of thumb

- **Compare two services every time.** Fees and promotional rates change monthly.
- **Bank-funded transfers beat card-funded ones.** Paying with a credit card often adds a cash-advance fee from your card issuer on top.
- **Larger, less frequent transfers usually beat small weekly ones**, since many fees are flat.
- **Never send through informal channels.** The "raise" someone offers on the street rate isn't worth the risk of losing the entire transfer.
