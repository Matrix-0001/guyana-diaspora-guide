ExxonMobil's discoveries turned Guyana into one of the fastest-growing economies on earth, and every Guyanese WhatsApp group has the same recurring thread: *should I move back for the boom?* Here's a grounded look at what the opportunity actually is — and isn't.

## Where the jobs actually are

**Directly offshore** — the FPSOs (floating production vessels) and drillships need operations technicians, marine crew, HSE officers, engineers, medics, caterers. These roles are real but competitive and often filled through international contractors (SBM Offshore, SLB, Halliburton, Saipem, and others) with experience requirements. If you already work oil and gas in Alberta, Texas, or the North Sea, you're the target profile.

**The onshore supply chain** — shorebases, logistics, warehousing, fabrication, transport, customs brokerage. This is the layer the boom multiplied fastest, and it hires across skill levels.

**Professional services** — accounting, law, banking, insurance, IT, project management, HSE consulting. International firms and expanding local ones compete for qualified people; diaspora professionals with recognized certifications (CPA, ACCA, PMP, engineering licenses) are in demand.

**Construction and real estate** — hotels, offices, housing schemes, roads, the new Demerara River bridge and gas-to-energy projects. Skilled trades, quantity surveyors, and construction managers do well.

**Everything else a boomtown needs** — healthcare, education, hospitality, food service, security. Georgetown's growth pulls the whole labor market.

## Your diaspora advantage: the Local Content Act

Guyana's **Local Content Act (2021)** requires oil companies and their contractors to procure a long schedule of services from **Guyanese nationals and Guyanese-owned companies**, and to prioritize Guyanese for employment. Two implications:

1. **As an employee**, your Guyanese citizenship (dual counts) plus foreign experience is precisely what companies need to hit local content targets while getting international-standard skills.
2. **As a business owner**, a Guyanese-owned company (majority Guyanese ownership and staffing, registered with the Local Content Secretariat) gets preferential access to contracts — catering, transport, IT support, maintenance, training. Some of the best diaspora outcomes are businesses serving the sector, not jobs inside it.

If you're returning permanently, pair the move with the [remigrant scheme](/articles/guyana-remigrant-scheme-guide/) for the duty-free concessions — the two decisions belong in the same plan.

## How to job-hunt from abroad

- **Target the contractors, not just Exxon.** The operators' websites list few roles; the contractor ecosystem (drilling, marine, logistics, catering, HSE) does the volume hiring
- **Use the local channels** — Guyanese job boards, newspaper listings, and LinkedIn with your location interest set; Guyana's professional circles are small and referrals carry weight
- **Get credentials recognized early** — licenses and certificates from the US/Canada/UK generally translate well, but bring documentation
- **Line up documents** as if it were an immigration case: [passport current](/articles/renew-guyana-passport-abroad/), [birth certificate clean](/articles/guyana-birth-certificate-from-abroad/), [police clearance](/articles/police-clearance-certificate-guyana/) if employers ask, TIN for payroll
- **Visit before you leap.** A two-week reconnaissance trip — meeting employers, checking rents, feeling out daily life — beats a hundred WhatsApp opinions

## The honest trade-offs

- **Georgetown's cost of living has jumped** — rents especially. Negotiate pay with current prices in mind, not memories
- **Salaries below the top technical tier often trail North American pay** — the move can still win on cost of living, family, and opportunity, but run the numbers
- **Booms concentrate** — the surge is strongest in Georgetown and along the coast corridors; opportunity elsewhere in the country is growing more slowly
- **Bring patience for process** — utilities, banking, bureaucracy move at their own pace, boom or not

The boom is real, the diaspora advantage under the Local Content Act is real, and so is the adjustment. The returnees who thrive treat it as a researched relocation, not a gold rush.
