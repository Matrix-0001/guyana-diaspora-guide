For many Guyanese, the hardest part of visiting family abroad isn't the flight — it's the visa interview. Here's how the B1/B2 visitor visa process works at the US Embassy in Georgetown, and what actually matters at the window.

## The process in brief

1. **Complete the DS-160** online — the long application form. Be accurate and consistent; your answers are checked against past applications.
2. **Pay the visa application (MRV) fee** — US$185 for a visitor visa (confirm the current amount when you apply).
3. **Book your interview** through the official appointment system. Wait times for an appointment in Georgetown vary from weeks to many months depending on demand — check early.
4. **Attend the interview** at the US Embassy on Young and Duke Streets, Kingston, Georgetown.

Beware of "agents" who offer to fill out forms or promise visas for a fee. The embassy repeatedly warns against them, and errors or fabrications on a DS-160 filed in your name follow you for life.

## What the officer is really assessing

Under US law, every visitor applicant is presumed to be an intending immigrant until they prove otherwise. The interview is about one question: **will this person return to Guyana?** Officers look at your ties:

- **Employment or a business** in Guyana — steady income matters more than a big number
- **Family who remain in Guyana** (spouse, children)
- **Property, land, or ongoing commitments**
- **Travel history** — previous trips abroad from which you returned on time

A relative in the States "sponsoring" the trip doesn't substitute for your own ties — sometimes it raises more questions than it answers.

## What to bring

The printed DS-160 confirmation and appointment letter, your passport, and fee receipt are required. Supporting documents (job letter, bank statements, land transport or title, business registration) are often not asked for — interviews are short — but carry them anyway, organized, in case they are.

## Answering questions at the window

- **Answer only what's asked**, briefly and truthfully.
- **Don't recite a script.** Officers interview hundreds of Guyanese applicants; rehearsed answers stand out.
- **Never lie about relatives in the US** or past overstays — the embassy has records, and a misrepresentation finding is a long-term bar.

## If you're refused

Most refusals are under section 214(b) — insufficient ties. It's not a permanent black mark. You can reapply any time, but a new application only makes sense when something has **changed**: a new job, a business, property, family circumstances. Reapplying immediately with the same profile usually gets the same answer, and each attempt costs the full fee.

## Renewals

If you've held a US visa before, you may qualify for an **interview waiver (drop-off) renewal** depending on current embassy policy — check the appointment site, because eligibility rules change.
